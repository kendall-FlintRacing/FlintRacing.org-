Flint Racing and STEM

Build something bigger!

Beginning with our building workshops, and continuing with our annual race, the Flint Soap Box Derby renewed a community tradition in an exciting (and fast!) way. Join us as we transform our program into a year-round community hub for anyone who dares to think a little bigger—a place to build, explore, race and grow together.

Our Mission

The Flint Soap Box Derby’s mission is to provide hands-on STEM learning in a collaborative environment that builds community partnership. Through workshops, racing and year-round learning, we bring young people, families, schools and neighbors together to turn ideas into skills, friendships and opportunities for our community.

Home/Family Story

The memories we build on

A family story. A community future.

01 · Where it began

Learning by building together

Flint Racing began with Kevin’s experience building alongside his grandfather. That connection—learning by doing, solving problems together and taking pride in what you make—continues through our workshops and race days.

Before there was a program, there was time spent together. A project to work on. A problem to figure out. The chance to make something with your own hands—and someone beside you to share it with.

02 · Passing it on

More than what we make

That feeling belongs to the next generation, too. Our workshops and race days give young people room to ask questions, try an idea, make an adjustment and try again. Every build is a chance to grow in confidence—and to discover what teamwork can do.

The car is something you can see. The patience, pride and connections made along the way are the things we hope students carry with them.

03 · A community future

There’s room at the workbench

At our workshop space and Cronin Derby Downs, families, schools, volunteers and partners help young people build skills and find a place on a team.

What began with a family connection continues through a community. Each person who shares their time, cheers from the sidelines or helps a student take the next step adds another page to the story.

Built with family. Carried forward by community.

About Derby Racing

Everything you need to build, race and cheer at Cronin Derby Downs: how it works from registration to race day, what race day looks like, and the words you’ll hear while following each heat.

How it works

▾
  1. Send an inquiryAsk about a building workshop or about race day. Staff follow up by email.
  2. RegisterOnce your place is confirmed, we email the official All-American Soap Box Derby registration form to fill out and sign online.
  3. Build your carLocal schools build with us for six weeks. Out-of-town groups join three times: information night, shell reveal day, and impound and race day.

Race day

Hover for heats, divisions & more ▾
Learn more

The top half of the hill is the program race: check-in, the start, the VIP tent and the Team Zone. The bottom half is the community event: the Welcome Tent, general admission, concessions, merchandise and the finish line.

  1. Park and enter at the Welcome TentPark in your color-coded parking zone and follow the ribbons to the Welcome Tent for your wristband and the day’s program.
  2. Check in up the hillParticipants and volunteers head up the hill to the Check-in Tent to sign in and pick up their folder, swag bag and race day shirt.
  3. Weigh-in and team photoCoaches gather their team and head to weigh-in for the team photo. Chaperones set up the team’s tent or zone.
  4. Warm-ups in the Team ZoneWhile the driver and coach go to the start line for warm-ups, teams wait in the Team Zone making posters and cheers and reading through their folders.
  5. Opening ceremonyDrivers join Kevin on the track for the opening ceremony, and then the race begins!
  6. RacingThe coach and one chaperone help load the driver at the start, and the second chaperone stays with the team to cheer. After each run, drivers get a ride back to the top of the hill.
  7. Team lunchOnce a team is out of the bracket, the team eats lunch together in the VIP tent, then rejoins family for the rest of the race.
  8. Final wave and awardsAfter the final heat, every participant meets at the top of the hill and rides down the track together for a final wave. Medals and trophies are awarded. Leaving early? Pick up your medal at the Welcome Tent.

03Following the heats

Terminology

Heat
Two racers race each other twice. The lower combined time wins the heat.
Phase
One run down the hill. Every heat has two phases.
Lane
Each side of the track, Lane 1 or Lane 2. Racers swap lanes between phases.
Wheel swap
Racers trade wheels between phases so equipment differences cancel out.
On the ramp
The racers are in the starting ramps at the top of the hill, about to go.
Ready, set / They’re off
The starter is about to drop the ramps, and the cars are rolling.
Time differential
How far ahead the winner of a phase finished, in thousandths of a second.
Combined time
Both phase times added together. The lower combined time wins.
Running order
The order heats are raced, shown on the live race page.
Bracket
The chart showing who races whom and where winners and losers go next.
Winners side
Racers with no losses yet.
Consolation side
Racers with one loss. One more loss and they’re out.
Double elimination
Racers are out after two losses.
Championship heat
The final heat between the winners-side and consolation-side finalists.
Championship runoff
A second championship heat, raced only if the consolation-side finalist wins the first one.
Bye
A free pass to the next round when the bracket isn’t full.
Bye run
A practice run offered to racers who had a bye.
Lane violation
Crossing the center line. The racer loses the heat.
Line violation
Hitting the timer. The racer loses the heat.
Rerun
A phase raced again, for example after something gets on the track.
Forfeit
A racer who can’t race the heat. The other racer moves on.
Division champion
The winner of a division’s bracket, eligible to qualify for Akron.
Akron
Home of the FirstEnergy All-American Soap Box Derby World Championship, where local champions can race next.
Heat sheet
The official record of every heat: racers, lanes, phases, times and results.

How heats work

Two phases

Two racers start side by side from the ramps and race down the hill. That’s Phase 1. They swap lanes and race again in Phase 2.

Lane and wheel swap

Between phases, racers trade lanes and wheels (the 2/4/2 Progressive swap, or the Wheel Bank method), so neither the lane nor the wheels decide the race.

Lowest combined time wins

Each phase is timed to the thousandth of a second. The heat goes to the lower combined time. That’s the same as adding the two phase margins together.

Double elimination

Racers are out after two losses. If the consolation-side finalist wins the first championship heat, a runoff heat decides the champion.

Example

Car 12 wins Phase 1 by 0.062 seconds. Car 7 wins Phase 2 by 0.086 seconds. Car 7 wins the heat by 0.024 seconds.

Crossing the center line (lane violation) or hitting the timer (line violation) loses the heat. Interference on the track means the phase is rerun. Follow every heat live on the live race page.

Build a car

Our six-week build

  1. Week 1 · CommunityMeet the team, learn workshop safety and get to know your schedule.
  2. Week 2 · BuildBegin assembly: steering, brakes, axles and alignment.
  3. Week 3 · SightSee your designs on the returning shells and receive team shirts as they arrive.
  4. Week 4 · PersevereKeep going, work together and bring the build closer to the finish.
  5. Week 5 · PrepareCheck the build and get ready for final inspections.
  6. Week 6 · RaceCelebrate your work at the Soap Box Derby.

Out-of-town schools and groups join us three times instead: information night, shell reveal day, and impound and race day.

What goes into every car

  1. Steering stop and foot brace
  2. Kingpins, axles and washer stacks
  3. Steering hardware, pulleys and cables (double-clamped)
  4. Stabilizer assembly
  5. A new brake pad, brake pedal and cable
  6. Fixed and adjustable weights
  7. Axle airfoils
  8. Foam, shell and team design
  9. Required decals: car number, sponsors, race city and AASBD logo
  10. Floorboard finish: tung oil and/or wax

Build exactly to the official plans for your division. Inspectors check every item on the official inspection sheet, so the plans are your checklist. Watch the official build videos for Stock ↗ and Super Stock ↗.

    More than a finish line.

    Our track. Our community.

    Cronin Derby Downs is the home of Flint Racing’s Soap Box Derby track in Flint, Michigan. Here, young people bring their teamwork, creativity and hands-on learning to the starting line, with families, teachers and volunteers cheering them on.

    Our vision is a year-round community hub where people can walk, play, race, build and grow. Park improvements and expanded activities are part of that vision; we’ll share opportunities as they become available.

    From the starting line, since 1969.

    A Flint racing legacy.

    Flint’s Soap Box Derby tradition began on Cadillac Street in the 1930s. Mayor Donald R. Cronin championed a permanent place for young people to build and race, and Cronin Derby Downs opened in 1969 near Southwestern High School.

    Generations of racers made this hill part of Flint’s story. Today, Flint Racing & STEM carries that legacy forward—connecting the thrill of gravity racing with teamwork, hands-on learning and a shared future for the park.

    Privacy

    How information is used on the Flint Racing & STEM website.

    Information you provide

    Registration, inquiry and account forms collect the contact and program information requested on each form. Depending on the activity, this can include parent or guardian details, participant names, school, age, emergency contacts, pickup instructions and permissions. Please provide information only through the appropriate form and avoid including private participant details in public messages.

    How information is used

    Flint Racing & STEM uses this information to review requests, coordinate programs and events, maintain attendance and authorized pickup records, manage approved accounts, and communicate about participation. Administrative records and private participant details are separate from public news, galleries and race displays.

    Photos and program updates

    Photo and publication permissions are reviewed through the program’s forms and media workflow. Shared program access does not by itself authorize public publication of a photograph. Contact staff about a photo, permission or correction request.

    Payments and other services

    When available, online donations use a Stripe-hosted checkout. The website records donation status and related information received through that process. Third-party services have their own privacy notices. An optional donor mailing-address form is separate from email updates.

    Browser preferences and sessions

    The website uses browser storage and cookies for sign-in sessions, access codes and some operational tools. Shared devices should be signed out after use.

    Contact, corrections and questions

    Contact Flint Racing & STEM at info@flintsoapbox.org or through the Contact page to ask about your information, request a correction, discuss photo permissions, or ask about available deletion and retention options. Staff will review requests in the context of program, safety and recordkeeping needs.

    This notice describes the prelaunch website. Program staff should confirm retention periods, service providers and the final privacy contact before launch.

    Race center

    Every heat, result and racer. Updates on its own.

    New to derby racing? How to follow along
    1. Soap box cars have no engine. Racers roll down the hill on gravity alone.
    2. Two cars race side by side, one in Lane 1 and one in Lane 2. Each heat is two runs: the racers swap lanes for run 2, and the lowest total time wins.
    3. The lights go red when cars are on the ramp, amber when they’re set, and green when they’re off.
    4. Times are shown to the thousandth of a second. A bye means a racer moves on to the next round without racing.
    5. Racers compete in brackets by division. In double elimination a racer is out after two losses and keeps racing on the consolation side after the first.
